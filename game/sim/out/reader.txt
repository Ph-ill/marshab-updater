LOG PAGE 01 · DAY 1
  Diego calls home while the delay is still under a second. Someone tells a bad joke and Diego laughs on the beat, in real time, with everyone at the table. Afterwards Diego writes down who was there and what they ate, and folds the list into a flight-suit pocket.
  Dorian and Marco share the night watch over the fleet link. From low orbit Earth fills the window, its dark side strung with cities. Dorian holds up a thumb, and the planet spills out around it on every side. They talk about home as if it were down the road. For a few more days, it is.

LOG PAGE 02 · DAY 2
  Marco plays the same four bars on a travel guitar at the end of every watch, into the fleet channel, never the rest of the song. By the last top-off tanker seven ships are guessing at the fifth bar, seven different ways.
  Ships fly a few kilometres apart, one seat a ship, so no single failure takes two people; a visit bends the rule and is logged. Votes advise; the flight director decides. Every few weeks ships close up for a tethered crossing. Phill goes first, and logs it: "Recommended. Hold on."
  Fleet vote: Public affairs wants a fleet portrait: eight ships in one frame, with Earth filling the sky behind them. That means eight ships a hundred metres apart, and the propellant to get there. [Fly the formation / Politely decline]
  Phill keeps a pot of galley herbs alive under a spare lamp, pinching it back like the beds at home.

LOG PAGE 03 · DAY 3

  Michelle's mail from home carries a kitten count in the subject line, eleven one week, thirteen the next, one always out of frame.
  Phill and Marco read the water ledger aloud at shift change and signed it, as the checklist requires.
