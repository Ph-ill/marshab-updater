LOG PAGE 01 · DAY 1
  Jai calls home while the delay is still under a second. Someone tells a bad joke and Jai laughs on the beat, in real time, with everyone at the table. Afterwards Jai writes down who was there and what they ate, and folds the list into a flight-suit pocket.
  Phill and Jacques share the night watch over the fleet link. From low orbit Earth fills the window, its dark side strung with cities. Phill holds up a thumb, and the planet spills out around it on every side. They talk about home as if it were down the road. For a few more days, it is.

LOG PAGE 02 · DAY 2
  Michelle plays the same four bars on a travel guitar at the end of every watch, into the fleet channel, never the rest of the song. By the last top-off tanker six ships are guessing at the fifth bar, six different ways.
  Ships fly a few kilometres apart, one seat a ship, so no single failure takes two; a visit bends the rule and is logged. The next ship reads back every critical command; votes only advise the flight director. Every few weeks ships close up for a tethered crossing. Dorian goes first: "educational".
  Every data drop brings Michelle a photo of the foster litter at home, counted for the record, one always out of frame.

LOG PAGE 03 · DAY 3
  The galley screen on Kim's ship takes a 2.3-gigabyte update overnight and restarts at breakfast: the logo, a progress bar, the three-note chime, all over before Kim has counted to eleven. Then it logs out again. By departure the whole fleet is humming the chime.
  Phill keeps a pot of galley herbs alive under a spare lamp, pinching it back like the beds at home.
  Jai runs the hatch drill like a breach: stack up, check corners, clear the module, "room clear" before the list says it.
  Jacques answered mail from home: weather, a dog, a fence that needs mending.

LOG PAGE 04 · DAY 4
  Dorian plants the garden: six seed pods in a tray the size of a laptop, under a lamp the colour of a plum. Dorian labels each pod in tiny capitals and says good night to them on an open channel. Several people say good night back.
  Jacques's kit is squared away past any inspection: straps dressed, pens taped, every call answered "copy" first.
  Something hits Diego's ship hard enough to tick through the whole hull. Every pressure reading is green. Diego doesn't trust green.

LOG PAGE 05 · DAY 5
  Diego's morning EVA, Kim suited at the next ship's hatch, found a fingernail crater in a heat-shield tile, short of the bondline. The entry team clears it on one condition: Diego's ship flies entry a few tenths of a degree steeper, for a sharper peak and a shorter soak.
  Jai reads the manifest like a shopping list: water on Cargo 3, rover on Cargo 4. Write-offs: a grey relay, "thruster stuck open"; the last window's hopper, "tipped, beacon intermittent"; Cargo 2, drill spares, "lost on landing, scar in a low-res pass"; socks, "reason unknown".
  Diego cat-proofs the cabin out of habit: cables clipped, nothing on a ledge, latches double-thrown. No cats aboard.

LOG PAGE 06 · DAY 6
  The last tanker undocks at dawn. Seven ships light their engines for departure, and Kim feels weight come back. The timeline calls the burn COMMIT: after it, every way home runs past Mars. From here the log keeps one page for every five days; by the next page Kim can hide Earth behind a thumb.
  The CO₂ scrubber on Jacques's ship throws its fourth fault since launch. Jacques fixes it, then labels it "temperamental" in permanent marker, under a label in Starbase handwriting that already says "be nice to it".
  Fleet vote: Mission Control proposes moving the fleet's clocks two hours forward, to line up with the ground team's shifts. Phill is for it. Jacques has seen how this study ended last time. [Change the clocks / Keep fleet time]
  Private, for whoever looks after Kim: The attitude control on Kim's ship has a slow wobble only Kim seems to notice. A switch to the alternate gain set would probably settle it. Procedure says the guidance team signs off any switch.

  SIT WITH IT
  From the log: Second sol, halfway to Cargo 2 for the drill spares the water hangs on, Kim driving, Diego on the battery: forty-eight percent used. The reserve rule says turn round: there and back ends under the floor, unless Cargo 2 charges trucks. The waiver takes eighteen minutes: YOUR CALL. Kim drives on. Cargo 2 hears them and lights the port at its foot. In marker: "Slow charger. Sit with it." Diego plugs in at four percent. All afternoon the charge it kept for the cold runs into the rover. Its lamp is dimmer that night; the cold is closer. To Starbase: "Landed. All seven, and the eighth plugged in." From a loading bay: "Told you it was slow."
